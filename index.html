1. Request initiation
Request for new streetlight
Any resident, community board, elected official, or city agency can initiate a request for a new streetlight or relocation of an existing one via 311.
311 Community board City Council
DOT Division of Street Lighting Engineering
Receives and evaluates all requests. Assesses lighting needs against IES RP-8 (roadway and pedestrian lighting standard). Response within 12 weeks.
DOT
2. Decision point
Standard or distinctive fixture?
Standard lights (steel octagonal pole + LED cobra head) follow a simpler path. Distinctive/decorative lights require multi-agency approval and elected-official funding.
Standard light
DOT approves and issues work order
DOT engineers design the installation per the NYC Street Design Manual. Work order issued to a licensed NYC electrical contractor and to Con Edison for power supply.
DOT Con Edison
Physical installation
Contractor installs pole, conduit (minimum 18-inch depth per Highway Rules Section 2-20), and luminaire. Con Edison provides the electrical connection. DOT's Electrical Inspection Division verifies the work.
DOT Con Edison
Distinctive / decorative light
Multi-agency approval required
DOT submits proposal on behalf of the applicant to the Public Design Commission (or Local Preservation Commission) and OMB. Fixtures must be sympathetic to neighborhood character.
Public Design Commission OMB DOT
Elected official funding secured
Distinctive lighting must be funded through elected officials -- City Council members or borough presidents via discretionary capital funds.
City Council
Installation (same process)
Once all approvals clear, same physical installation process: licensed contractor + Con Edison connection + DOT inspection.
DOT Con Edison
Streetlight operational
DOT assumes ownership and maintenance responsibility. Con Edison bills DOT for electricity (estimated usage). Light enters the city's ~400,000-unit inventory. All service requests tracked on NYC Open Data.
1. Report
Resident reports streetlight issue
Via 311 (phone, web portal, app) or DOT's dedicated Street Lighting Repair Form. No contact info required, but email gets confirmation. Report categories: burned out, flickering, on during the day, leaning pole, exposed wires.
311
Emergency or non-emergency?
Downed power lines = call 911. Fallen pole or active electrical hazard = emergency. Burned out / flickering = non-emergency.
2. Response
Emergency (fallen pole, hazard)
4-hour response
DOT's contracted crew must respond within 4 hours. Area is secured and temporary measures taken.
DOT
Non-emergency (burned out, flickering)
10-day response
DOT's contracted crew must respond within 10 business days. Permanent repair within 90-day permit period.
DOT
3. Diagnosis and repair
What's the root cause?
DOT contractor diagnoses the issue on site.
DOT-side issue
DOT contractor repairs
Bulb/LED replacement, wiring fix, conduit repair, or pole replacement. Must complete permanent repair within 90-day permit period.
DOT
Utility-side issue
Referred to Con Edison
Power supply problems, nearby construction impacts, or utility feed issues are escalated to Con Edison for resolution.
Con Edison
If still out after 90 days
Resident should contact their Borough Commissioner Office directly. NYC Council analysis has found DOT resolution reporting can be inconsistent around deadline marks.

Funding sources
City expense budget
Covers DOT's ~$50M annual electricity bill to Con Edison (295.5M kWh estimated usage, per DOT's "Green Light" report) and routine maintenance contracts with licensed electrical contractors.
OMB DOT
City capital budget
Funds larger infrastructure: new pole installations, underground conduit, major system upgrades. Subject to OMB review and City Council approval.
OMB City Council
Council member discretionary funds
Each of 51 Council members receives an annual allocation for local needs. Can direct capital funds toward streetlight projects. Required for all distinctive/decorative lighting.
City Council
Community board budget requests
Community boards submit annual requests to upgrade illumination in their districts. Advisory only — not binding, but influences budget priorities.
Community board
NYPA financing (LED conversion)
New York Power Authority provided upfront financing for the statewide LED conversion. Municipalities repay from energy savings generated by reduced electricity use of LED fixtures.
NYPA
Private expense (relocations)
When a private party requests relocation of an existing streetlight, all costs are borne by the applicant. Must use a licensed NYC electrical contractor and coordinate with Con Edison. See DOT conditions.
Con Edison
New mandate: sidewalk lighting (effective Jan. 1, 2027)
300 commercial blocks per year
City Council passed a law requiring DOT to install pedestrian-oriented lighting on at least 300 commercial blocks per year until every commercially zoned block meets a 1-footcandle average standard. Estimated cost: $27M/year (~$90K per block). Engineering note: DOT has flagged the 1-footcandle standard as potentially excessive — IES RP-8 recommends ~0.9 fc for high-activity sidewalks.

NYC LED conversion timeline
~2013
DOT announces plan to replace 250,000 streetlights with LEDs — the largest municipal LED conversion in the country.
2014-2018
Rollout in waves of ~80,000 lights. Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx completed by 2018. Total project cost: $76.5 million.
2019
NYPA launches "Smart Street Lighting NY" statewide program in partnership with Signify (Philips). Over 130 municipalities participate.
2020
NYPA launches maintenance service for municipalities using NYPA's asset management controls.
August 2023
New York State achieves goal of 500,000 LED streetlights — nearly three years ahead of the end-of-2025 target.

Agency roles
NYC Department of Transportation (DOT)
Primary owner and operator. The Division of Street Lighting Engineering handles all planning, engineering, permitting, installation approvals, and maintenance contracting. DOT owns the poles and fixtures (not Con Edison). Contracts with private electrical firms for physical work. Regulated by NYC Highway Rules Section 2-20 and the NYC Street Design Manual.
DOT
Con Edison
Electricity provider only. Supplies power and installs electrical connections. Does not own poles or fixtures. Billed ~$50M/year by DOT for estimated usage (per DOT's "Green Light" report).
Con Edison
Office of Management and Budget
Reviews and approves distinctive lighting proposals. Controls capital and expense budget allocations for streetlight infrastructure.
OMB
Public Design Commission
Must approve all non-standard / distinctive streetlight designs before installation. Encourages fixtures sympathetic to neighborhood architectural and historic character.
Public Design Commission
City Council
51 members with discretionary capital funds. Required funder for all distinctive lighting. Passed the 2027 sidewalk lighting mandate (300 blocks/year).
City Council
Community boards
Advisory role. Submit annual budget requests for lighting upgrades. Receive and forward resident complaints. No direct approval authority.
Community board
New York Power Authority (NYPA)
Runs the statewide Smart Street Lighting NY program. Provides upfront financing for LED conversions; municipalities repay from energy savings.
NYPA
Regulatory framework
NYC Highway Rules, Section 2-20
"Street Light and Power" — governs conduit specs (minimum 18-inch depth), conductor insulation, transformer grounding, and mechanical protection (within 8 ft of ground).
NYC Street Design Manual
Comprehensive reference for acceptable pole types, luminaire specifications, and design standards. Published by DOT at nycstreetdesign.info.
Standard equipment
Standard street light (SSL)
Steel octagonal pole (30 ft tall, 8 ft arm) with LED cobra head luminaire. Highway spec: 110W max, IES Type II or III distribution.
Distinctive / Type M lamppost
Decorative one-piece base and shaft, intermediate shaft, mast arm, scroll and finial. 21" diameter round base. Requires PDC approval.
